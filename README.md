# MLforFloodPrediction

Research repository for a MICAI 2026 paper submission focused on predictive flood modeling in Mexico City using hydro-meteorological, temporal, spatial, and urban infrastructure data. The project explores different machine learning approaches for early warning and urban risk management systems.

# Abstract. 
Flood prediction in Mexico City has emerged as a critical challenge due to the interaction of climatic, hydrological, urban, and infrastructural factors that increase territorial exposure and vulnerability. Flood risk in the Valley of Mexico depends not only on precipitation intensity but also on infiltration loss, urban expansion, topography, drainage performance, land-use change, subsidence, and basin degradation.

Despite these contributions, important limitations remain. Much of the existing literature emphasizes risk assessment and scenario simulation rather than prediction. In this context, this work proposes a comprehensive analysis of five different models, ranging from straightforward methods such as logistic regression, or more tree-based techniques such as XGBoost, to sophisticated deep learning models such as LTSM, GRU, and TCN. And by combining real-time meteorological information with alerts for hydrological failures and time-lagged features that capture hydrological memory, we've assessed these models
